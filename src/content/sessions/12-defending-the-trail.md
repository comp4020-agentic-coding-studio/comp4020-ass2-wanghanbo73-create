## Before the session

There is no instructor framing of what matters in the assigned system beyond
a failure report. Everything else — which evidence class to reach for, what
counts as sufficient — is your call, and has been building toward this all
semester.

## The synthesis exercise

Before the Final itself, this session runs a smaller rehearsal: a system
neither you nor anyone else in the room has seen, scoped small enough and
timed short enough that completing a full repair isn't realistic. What you
produce instead is a condensed trail — starting observations, the
hypotheses you held, why you chose the evidence you gathered, the moment
your uncertainty actually narrowed, what you'd intervene with, how you'd
verify it, and what would still be uncertain if you stopped here. There is
no recipe for this; the exercise exists to rehearse producing a defensible
chain of reasoning under a real time limit, not to produce a fix.

## What the defence actually tests

The live defence — for this exercise and for the Final — is not a
presentation to rehearse until it sounds polished. A questioner will ask
things like why you trusted a particular piece of evidence, what result
would have changed your conclusion, which alternative you ruled out and how,
and what your verification does and doesn't prove. There is no credit for a
narrative that sounds complete; there is credit for a trail that survives
being pushed on, including the parts you'd have to admit are still
unverified.

## In the session

You present your observation-to-verification trail — first for the
synthesis exercise, then, in the Final itself, for a previously unseen
system assigned individually — to a questioner who probes for unstated
assumptions, hypotheses you never actually tested, and verification claims
that don't hold up. The defence is oral and live; the questions are not
supplied in advance.

## Afterwards

This is the final teaching session of the semester. What leaves the room is
whatever your investigation trail could actually withstand under challenge —
not a polished report of what you'd like it to have been.
