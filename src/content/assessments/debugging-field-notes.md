> Observe precisely, hypothesise a cause you could be wrong about, and show
> your work before you know whether you were right.

This is the semester's first graded work, and it is deliberately low-risk:
four short entries, one per week, each tied to that week's specific skill —
observation, hypothesis and disconfirmation, structural reading, runtime
evidence. None of the systems involved is large enough to hide behind.

The mark does not reward reaching a correct answer by any route. A
hypothesis that was never actually falsifiable, or a disconfirming test
written up after the result was already known, scores lower than an entry
that commits to a test, runs it, and reports that the test refuted the
original guess.

## What you submit

Four entries, one after each of Weeks 1–4, each recording the observation,
hypothesis, disconfirming test, evidence, and — where relevant — an
intervention and its verification, in that order. The order is the point:
an entry is judged partly on whether its own internal chronology could have
happened the way it claims.

## How it's marked

Early weighting sits on observation, hypothesis formation, and genuine
disconfirmation, rather than on the later stages of the cycle — those matter
more once Assessments 2–4 raise the stakes on them.
