> Here is a system you have never seen, and a report of what a user
> observed. Investigate it, fix what needs fixing, and be ready to explain
> every step.

This is the course's integrative assessment. You receive a system
consistent with Weeks 10–11's conditions — multi-component, incompletely
documented, and containing at least one source of operational uncertainty —
with a failure report but no framing of which subsystem, evidence class, or
tool is likely to matter. It is not Broken Service Investigation at a larger
scale: the difference is the absence of cueing, not the size of the
repository.

## What you submit

An early checkpoint of your initial observations and planned next step; a
full evidence trail; an explicit account of any evidence source you judged
unreliable and why; a diagnosis that addresses alternatives; a scoped
intervention with a verification argument tied to the diagnosed cause; a
statement of remaining uncertainty; and a short live defence in which a
questioner probes your observations, hypotheses, evidence choices,
exclusions, intervention scope, and verification — the questions are not
supplied in advance.

## How it's marked

Reaching a working final state through undirected changes, without being
able to explain why alternatives were excluded or why the verification
demonstrates the diagnosed cause is resolved, does not earn a high mark on
that basis alone. A rigorous investigation with a justified, safely-scoped
intervention can still score well if genuine system complexity prevents a
complete repair — provided the remaining uncertainty is stated plainly
rather than hidden. Defence carries the single largest weight, because it
tests whether the investigation is an argument you understand, not a
narrative assembled after the fact.
